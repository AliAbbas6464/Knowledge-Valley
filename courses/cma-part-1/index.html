Part 1 Overview

CMA USA Part 1 — Financial Planning, Performance & Analytics

CMA USA Part 1 is the first of the two exams that make up the US CMA certification. It focuses on Financial Planning, Performance and Analytics — the areas of management accounting concerned with how organizations plan, budget, measure performance and manage cost, backed by strong internal controls and the use of data and technology.

The subject brings together six areas: external financial reporting decisions, planning, budgeting and forecasting, performance management, cost management, internal controls, and technology and analytics. Together, they test how well a candidate can both understand accounting concepts and apply them to real business decisions. In Detail

CMA USA Part 1 Subjects

What each topic actually covers, beyond the syllabus title.

📑

External Financial Reporting Decisions 15%

How financial statements are prepared and presented, and how choices in recognition, measurement and disclosure affect the numbers a business reports. Includes the key differences between GAAP and IFRS that candidates are expected to know.

📅

Planning, Budgeting, and Forecasting 20%

Building an annual budget from strategic goals down to operating detail, forecasting techniques for revenue and cost, and the planning methodologies organizations use to translate strategy into a workable financial plan.

📈

Performance Management 20%

Measuring how well a business, team or division is actually performing against plan — variance analysis, responsibility centers, and performance measures including the balanced scorecard.

💰

Cost Management 15%

Costing systems and measurement concepts, how overhead and supply-chain costs are allocated and controlled, and techniques used to improve business processes and reduce waste.

🛡️

Internal Controls 15%

Governance, risk and compliance frameworks, how internal control systems are designed and assessed, and the role of internal audit in detecting and preventing fraud.

💻

Technology and Analytics 15%

How information systems and data governance support decision-making, and the use of descriptive, predictive and prescriptive analytics tools across an organization.

Exam Format

CMA USA Part 1 Exam Format

CMA Part 1 is a four-hour exam made up of two sections.

  • 100 multiple-choice questions, covering all six topic areas above. You must score at least 50% on this section to move on to the next.
  • An application section, which follows the multiple-choice questions.
  • From the September/October 2026 testing window, English-language CMA exams use case-based questions (CBQs) in this application section rather than essay questions. Each case-based question presents a short business scenario (around 250 words) followed by a set of structured questions — drag-and-drop, fill-in-the-blank, select-from-a-list and calculations — rather than a written essay response.
  • The topics tested, exam length and overall difficulty are not changing — only the format of the application section has moved from essay writing to case-based questions.

Study Approach

How to Prepare for CMA USA Part 1

A practical, six-stage process that works whether you're studying alongside a job or full-time.

1

Learn

Go through each topic conceptually first — what it means and why it matters — before trying to memorize formulas or definitions.

2

Practice

Work through topic-wise questions right after learning a topic, while the concept is still fresh, rather than saving all practice for the end.

3

Test

Take a short test on that topic to check what actually stuck, not just what you recognize when you see it again.

4

Analyze

Review your test results honestly — which areas are consistently weak, and which mistakes are careless versus conceptual.

5

Revise

Go back to the specific weak areas identified in your analysis, rather than re-reading everything from the start.

6

Mock Exam

Once all topics are covered, take full-length mock exams under timed conditions to build stamina and get comfortable with the real exam format.

A Fair Question

Is CMA USA Part 1 Difficult?

How difficult Part 1 feels depends largely on your accounting background, how much time you can commit each week, and how consistently you practice rather than just read. A candidate coming from a strong accounting or finance degree will find some topics — like external financial reporting — more familiar than someone approaching the material for the first time.

What makes Part 1 genuinely demanding is its range: it moves from financial reporting and budgeting into performance measurement, cost management, internal controls, and analytics — six distinct areas, each requiring both conceptual understanding and the ability to apply that understanding to a scenario rather than just recall a definition.

This is why structured preparation matters more than raw study hours. Working through topic-wise practice questions, testing yourself regularly, and taking full mock exams under timed conditions builds the kind of applied understanding the exam actually checks for. There's no shortcut or guarantee — but a clear plan and consistent practice make the difference between studying and being exam-ready.
