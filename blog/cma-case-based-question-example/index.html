Quick answer

A CMA case-based question (CBQ) is a short business case with an exhibit, followed by up to seven questions (Gleim). You answer by typing a number, selecting from a list, dragging items into boxes or selecting several answers.

This page is a CMA CBQ practice example with answers. Below is a full case you can solve on screen. After each question, press Watch the solution to see the working build step by step. The case is about cost-volume-profit analysis (Part 1) and is an original Knowledge Valley example, not an IMA question.

Most pages about CMA case-based questions (CBQs) describe the format and then stop. This one lets you do one. If you want the background first, read our guide to CMA case-based questions vs essays. For the method, see how to answer CMA case-based questions. The format is the same for candidates testing in Saudi Arabia and the UAE.

Try before you watch

You learn far more from a wrong answer you tried than from a solution you only read. Answer first, even if you are unsure. There is no penalty on this screen, and IMA says there is none for wrong answers in the real exam.

Case and exhibit (tap to hide)

Harbor Foods (a fictional company) bottles premium olive oil. It sells each bottle for $20 and sells about 40,000 bottles a year on its current filling line.

Management is considering a new automatic filling line. The new line would lower the variable cost per bottle but needs more fixed costs, mainly depreciation and maintenance. The sales manager is not sure that sales will stay at 40,000 bottles, so the finance team has been asked to compare the two lines. Ignore taxes. All figures are annual.

How to solve a CMA case-based question in 5 steps

  1. Scan the questions firstNote what each one asks and in which format, so you know what to look for in the case.
  2. Read the case and the exhibit for the figures you needMark the numbers. Ignore the rest.
  3. Name the concept before you calculateFor example, break-even point, margin of safety or operating income at a given volume.
  4. Answer in the format the screen asks forType a plain number, choose from the list, place the items, or select exactly the number of answers requested.
  5. Check the unit, the sign and the rounding, then move onIf a case is taking much more than 30 minutes, move to the next one and come back if time allows.

The idea behind the case

This case is about cost-volume-profit (CVP) analysis, a topic in CMA Part 1. If you are preparing for it, see our CMA Part 1 live classes. The idea is simple: every bottle sold adds a contribution margin (price minus variable cost), and fixed costs must be covered before there is any profit.

  • Break-even point: fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit.
  • Margin of safety: how far sales can fall before profit reaches zero.
  • Choosing between cost structures: a line with higher fixed costs and lower variable costs earns more at high volumes and less at low volumes. The crossing point here is 45,000 bottles.

Real case-based questions in other topics follow the same pattern: read the exhibit, name the concept, calculate, then answer in the format the screen asks for.

FAQs

What does a CMA case-based question (CBQ) look like?

A CMA case-based question starts with a short business case. Gleim describes it as about 250 words, with exhibits such as financial information or charts, followed by up to seven questions. You answer by typing a number, selecting from a list, dragging items or selecting more than one answer. The example above follows this format.

How do I solve a CMA case-based question?

Use five steps. First, scan the questions to see what each one asks. Second, read the case and the exhibit for the figures you need. Third, name the concept, such as break-even point or margin of safety, before you calculate. Fourth, calculate and enter the answer in the format the screen asks for. Fifth, check the unit, the sign and the rounding, then move on. Our guide to how to answer CMA case-based questions explains each step.

Where can I practise CMA case-based questions with answers?

Start with the worked example on this page. It has six questions, one for each way of answering, with answers and an animated solution after each one. IMA also publishes sample case-based questions on its website, so try those next. Each part of our live course includes 30 case-based questions.

Is this an actual IMA case-based question?

No. It is an original example written by Knowledge Valley in the same format, so you can practise the screen actions and the method. Use it together with IMA’s own sample questions.

How do I enter a numerical answer in a CMA CBQ?

Gleim says you type a plain number, with no dollar signs or commas, and that the exam gives rounding instructions as needed. So 30,000 is typed as 30000. Read the line under each answer box before you type.

How many questions are in one CMA case?

Up to seven, according to Gleim. Each part of the exam has two cases after the 100 multiple-choice questions, and about one hour is allowed for both. Our example has six questions so you can practise every format in one case.

How long should one case-based question take?

About 30 minutes. You have about one hour for the two cases in each part, so we suggest 30 minutes each. Time yourself on this example: with six questions, aim for about 30 minutes in total, including reading the case.

How do drag-and-drop questions work in the CMA exam?

Gleim lists drag and drop as one of the case-based question formats. You match items to the places they belong. On our practice screen you can drag an item into a box, or tap an item and then tap a box, which works on a phone. Read what each box asks before you place anything.

Is there partial credit on CMA case-based questions?

Yes. IMA says case-based questions give partial credit, as the essays did, and that there is no penalty for wrong answers. IMA does not publish how partial credit is worked out for each question, so the score on our practice screen is only a teaching guide.

How do I find the break-even point and margin of safety in a CMA CBQ?

These are cost-volume-profit (CVP) questions, a CMA Part 1 topic. Contribution margin per unit = selling price − variable cost per unit. Break-even point in units = fixed costs ÷ contribution margin per unit. Margin of safety = (current sales − break-even sales) ÷ current sales. In our example, $240,000 ÷ $8 = 30,000 bottles, and (40,000 − 30,000) ÷ 40,000 = 25%.

How do I choose between two cost structures in a CMA CBQ?

Find the sales level where both options give the same operating income, by setting the two profit equations equal. Here 8Q − 240,000 = 10Q − 330,000, so Q = 45,000 bottles. Below that volume the option with the lower fixed costs earns more. Above it, the option with the lower variable cost per unit earns more.

Are CMA case-based questions the same in Saudi Arabia and the UAE?

Yes. IMA lists the Middle East among the regions where case-based questions replaced essays on English exams from the September/October 2026 window, so candidates in Saudi Arabia and the UAE answer the same format as everyone else. Mainland China, Taiwan and Japan are the exceptions.
