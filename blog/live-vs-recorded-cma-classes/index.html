Quick answer

For most working professionals, live CMA classes work better than recorded ones. A live class gives you a fixed time that starts the habit, a teacher who answers your question on the spot, feedback on your practice, and a group that keeps you moving. Recorded classes give flexibility, but they depend entirely on your own routine, and questions wait for a reply.

Live CMA classes are online lessons taught in real time at a set hour, where you can ask the teacher questions. Recorded CMA classes are lessons filmed in advance that you watch at any time.

This guide is for finance professionals in the UAE, Saudi Arabia (KSA) and the wider Gulf who work full time and want to pass the US CMA. A CMA takes months of steady study, so the way you learn matters. Here is what each format gives you, what the research says, and how to choose.

Recorded classes have one clear strength: you can replay a lesson. For most working professionals, though, the structure of a live class matters more, because the exam takes months of steady work.

Why are live CMA classes better for working professionals?

A fixed time
Builds the habit

After a long workday it is easy to put a video off. A class at a set hour gets you to start.

Answers in the moment
No waiting

You ask while the topic is fresh. A doubt is cleared in minutes, not left for days.

Feedback on your practice
Learn from errors

Case-based questions need a reviewer. A teacher can show where marks are lost and why.

  • A steady pace. The class moves on every week, so you finish each part in good time. You have three years to pass both parts after the entrance fee, and steady weeks keep you inside that window.
  • A teacher who adapts. In a live class the teacher sees where the group is stuck and explains the topic again in another way.
  • A group to learn with. Other candidates share the same pressure. They ask questions that you had not thought of, and their progress keeps you going.
  • Practice built in. Each part at Knowledge Valley includes 30 case-based questions (CBQs) and 12 full mock exams.

What are the disadvantages of recorded CMA classes?

Recorded lessons can be well made. The limits come from how people study with them:

  • The routine is left to you. Without a set time, a lesson is easy to postpone. One missed week becomes two, and the restart gets harder.
  • Questions wait. A doubt that stays open for days can slow the next topic, because CMA topics build on each other.
  • Watching is not the same as practising. It is easy to watch a lesson and feel that you know it, until a case-based question tests you. Live classes put practice in the room.
  • You study mostly alone. There is no group and little feedback on your own answers.

This is why we suggest recorded material as a revision aid after a live class, not as the main way to learn.

What does research say about live and recorded online learning?

These studies are about online learning in general, not only the CMA. They show that the schedule makes the clearest difference.

  • A 2021 review of studies (Martin and others) found a small effect in favour of live online learning for cognitive outcomes.
  • A 2023 review of 14 studies found recorded learning slightly better for knowledge, but the effect was trivial in size. So, learning results are close.
  • In one introductory programming course taught in two versions, the fixed-schedule version kept 45% of students and the self-paced version kept 13%.
  • In a review of free open online courses, the average completion rate was 6.8%. These are free courses, so paid courses differ, but it shows how many people stop when no one sets the pace.

What do we take from this? Both formats can teach. The difference is who keeps going. A CMA takes about 300 hours of study and IMA’s last published pass rate was near 45%, so keeping going is what counts.

Studying the CMA in the Gulf evening

Many of our students work full days in the UAE, Saudi Arabia (KSA) and nearby countries. The evening is when they can study. That is why our live classes run in the evening on Saudi time. The UAE is one hour ahead of Saudi Arabia, so one evening slot works for both. See how the CMA is viewed in the UAE and in Saudi Arabia (KSA), or look at our live CMA Part 1 and CMA Part 2 classes.

If you pick recorded classes, give your study a fixed place in the week in the same way. Treat the video as a class that starts at a set time, not as something to watch when you find a gap.

Getting the most from each format

If you study live
  • Protect the class time like a meeting
  • Write down your questions before class
  • Practise questions the same week
  • Review the notes within two days
If you study recorded
  • Book fixed study hours in your calendar
  • Set a finish date for each topic
  • Pause the video to try the question yourself
  • Find a person or a forum to answer doubts

Asking for help is the part that students often skip. In a live class, you can ask straight away. In a recorded course, make sure there is a clear way to ask and a clear time for the answer. Whatever the format, practise with case-based questions early. Read how to answer CMA case-based questions to see the method.

How do you choose between live and recorded CMA classes?

  1. Look at your week. If you can protect one or two evenings, live classes fit and give you the best structure. Only if your hours truly change from week to week, consider recorded lessons and add fixed study hours.
  2. Be honest about discipline. Working professionals are tired in the evening. A live class gives you a reason to start. With recorded lessons, you must create that reason yourself.
  3. Think about questions. The CMA has many topics that build on each other. Live classes clear doubts the same day.
  4. See a class before you pay. Ask for a sample class or an orientation, and ask how questions are answered. We offer a free orientation class.
  5. Check the practice. Whatever the format, look for case-based questions and full mock exams. Each part at Knowledge Valley includes 30 case-based questions (CBQs) and 12 full mock exams.

FAQs

Are live CMA classes better than recorded classes?

For most working professionals, yes. Live classes give you a fixed time that starts the habit, a teacher who answers your question in the moment, and a group that keeps you moving. Recorded classes give flexibility, but they depend on your own routine and your questions wait for a reply. The CMA takes about 300 hours of study over several months, so steady progress matters most. Live classes are built to give that.

Can I pass the CMA with recorded classes?

Yes, some candidates do. It takes a strong routine: fixed study hours, practice questions after every lesson, mock exams, and a way to get doubts answered. Many working professionals find it easier to keep that routine with a live class.

What are the disadvantages of recorded CMA classes?

Recorded classes leave the routine to you, so lessons are easy to postpone and the gap grows. Questions wait for a reply, so a doubt can sit for days. You study mostly alone, and nobody checks your case-based answers as you go. They are best used for revision after a live class.

Why choose live CMA classes?

Live classes give you a set time each week, answers in the moment, feedback on your practice and a group to learn with. The teacher can see where the class is struggling and explain it again. This structure helps working professionals keep going until the exam.

Is live CMA coaching worth it for a working professional?

It is worth it if you want structure, answers in the moment and a group to learn with. A CMA takes about 300 hours of study, and most candidates work full time, so a set evening class helps many people keep going. Ask for a sample class first, and check that the course includes case-based questions and full mock exams.

What are live CMA classes?

Live CMA classes are lessons taught online in real time at a set hour. You join with the teacher and other candidates, and you can ask questions during the class. Knowledge Valley teaches live only, in the evening on Saudi time.

How many hours does the CMA take to study?

Becker, a CMA review provider, says candidates generally need about 170 hours for Part 1 and 130 hours for Part 2. That is about 300 hours in total. Spread over several months, it is a few hours a week alongside a job.

What is the CMA pass rate?

IMA last published a global pass rate in 2020, and the figure was about 45% across Part 1 and Part 2. With a pass rate like this, steady study and practice matter more than the format you pick.

Can I work full time and study the CMA?

Yes. Most CMA candidates work. Plan around your job: study on fixed evenings and keep weekends for practice questions and mock exams. Our live classes run in the evening on Saudi time so that you can attend after work.

Is the evening time right for both the UAE and Saudi Arabia?

Yes. The UAE is one hour ahead of Saudi Arabia (UAE time is UTC+4 and Saudi time is UTC+3), so an evening class works for both. Check the batch time before you enrol.

How do I stay consistent when I study the CMA?

Pick the same days and hours each week, set a target for each topic, and practise questions after every lesson. A fixed schedule, from a live class or from your own calendar, is the habit that matters most.
