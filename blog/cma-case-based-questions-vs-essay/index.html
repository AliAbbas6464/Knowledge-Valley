CMA Case-Based Questions (CBQs) vs Essays: What Changed and What to Do

Quick answer

From the September/October 2026 window, the CMA exam has no essays in the Middle East. Each part now ends with two case-based questions (CBQs) instead of two essays.

The 100 multiple-choice questions, the topics, the four-hour exam, the 25% weight of this section and the fees stay the same. You answer by selecting, dragging and entering numbers on screen, not by typing paragraphs.

If you are planning a CMA exam in Saudi Arabia, the UAE or elsewhere in the Gulf, the question is simple: what is different in the new CMA exam format, and does it change how you study? This guide answers both, using IMA’s own announcement as the main source.

IMA (the Institute of Management Accountants) awards the CMA (US) credential. It announced the change to the second section of each part, and that is the only part of the exam that moved.

What changed in the CMA exam in 2026, and when

IMA rolled the change out across the three 2026 testing windows. Here is the timeline in one place.

CMA essay-to-CBQ timeline for English exams, including the Middle East
Testing windowSecond section format
January/February 2026Two essays. Registration for the CBQ option opened.
May/June 2026Candidates chose essays or case-based questions for each part. IMA called this the final window where essays were offered for the English exam.
September/October 2026 and laterCase-based questions only. Essays are no longer available in the Middle East, Europe, the Americas, Asia Pacific and Africa.
Key point

Mainland China, Taiwan and Japan still offer essays. Every Gulf candidate now sits the case-based version. The next window, January/February 2027, is case-based as well.

The last two rows are the real shift. Writing speed, grammar and essay structure stop mattering. Reading accuracy and calculation accuracy matter more.

What a case-based question looks like

A case-based question (CBQ) is a short business case followed by a set of linked questions that you answer by selecting, dragging or entering numbers on screen. Each one starts with a short business case, followed by a group of questions about that same case. Gleim describes each case as about 250 words, supported by exhibits such as financial information or charts, with up to seven questions per case. IMA publishes sample case-based questions on its website, so check those for the exact layout.

Gleim lists these question types:

  • Drag-and-drop: match items to the right category.
  • Select from a list: choose the correct option from a drop-down menu.
  • Multiple response: pick every answer that applies.
  • Numerical entry: calculate and type the number.
Why it matters

One case can cover several linked decisions. A wrong number early on can carry into later questions, so read the exhibits first and check your first calculation before you build on it.

What did not change

IMA says exam length, pricing and credential recognition remain unchanged. In practical terms:

  • Each part is still a four-hour, computer-based exam taken at a Prometric test centre.
  • Each part still has 100 multiple-choice questions (about three hours), followed by the case-based section (about one hour). MCQs are 75% of the score and case-based questions are 25%.
  • You still need at least 50% on the multiple-choice section to reach the case-based questions.
  • The passing score is still 360 out of 500.
  • The syllabus, topics and learning outcomes are the same, so your existing study material is still valid for the content.

The change applies to CMA Part 1 and CMA Part 2 in the same way. Each part ends with two case-based questions on that part’s own topics. If you want to see how a live course plans each part, read about our CMA Part 1 live classes and CMA Part 2 live classes.

Watch out

Because the case-based section opens only after you clear the 50% MCQ gate, the multiple-choice questions still decide whether your case-based answers are counted at all. Do not shift all your practice time to the new format.

What this means if you test in Saudi Arabia or the UAE

IMA lists the Middle East among the regions where essays have ended on English exams. Whether you take the CMA exam in the UAE or in Saudi Arabia, the format is the same: there is no local exception and no choice to make. Your next step depends on where you are in your CMA journey. For the wider picture, see our guides to the US CMA in the UAE and the US CMA in Saudi Arabia.

Where you are now, and what to do
Your situationWhat to do
Not yet booked, planning January/February 2027 or laterPrepare for the case-based format from the start. You will not see essays.
Passed one part with essaysNothing to redo. Your pass counts. Prepare the other part for case-based questions and finish within your three-year window.
Studying from older essay-based notes or recordingsThe content is still right. Add case-based practice, because essay practice does not train drag-and-drop or numerical-entry questions. Ask your course how it covers them.
Failed an attempt that included essaysYour retake will be case-based. Review the section you struggled with, then practise the new format.

There is one advantage for Gulf professionals who study in English as a second language. Typing a long written answer under time pressure is no longer part of the exam. You still need to read the case accurately, but you do not have to write fluent paragraphs.

How to prepare for the new CMA exam format in 5 steps

  1. Keep your MCQ accuracy firstYou need 50% on the multiple-choice section to unlock the case-based questions. Keep drilling MCQs as before.
  2. Try IMA’s sample case-based questions earlyDo this once in your first month, not in the last week. It shows you the screen layout and the question types.
  3. Practise in timed blocks of about 30 minutes per caseYou have about an hour for two cases. Time each case, then review where you lost minutes.
  4. Never leave an item blankIMA says partial credit applies and there is no penalty for a wrong answer. A reasoned guess beats a blank.
  5. Read the case with the questions in mindLook at what each question asks first, then read the case and mark the key numbers. Check each question’s command word: calculate, identify and select ask for different things.

Old essay questions are still useful for one thing: they teach you to pull the right numbers out of a long case. Use them for reading practice, not for writing practice.

What nobody has published yet

Many pages make claims about the new format. We kept to what IMA and Gleim publish, and this is what is and is not confirmed.

  • Confirmed by IMA: same topics, same difficulty level, same 25% weight, partial credit, and no change to exam length or pricing.
  • Not published by IMA: a detailed scoring method for case-based questions, and pass-rate figures for the new format. Treat any pass-rate claim for CBQs as an estimate.
  • Not found on IMA’s pages: a confirmed results timeline for the new format. Check the date in your own results email from IMA.

FAQs

Are there still essay questions on the CMA exam?

Not in the Middle East. From the September/October 2026 testing window, IMA no longer offers essays on English CMA exams delivered in the Americas, Europe, the Middle East, Asia Pacific and Africa. Mainland China, Taiwan and Japan are the exceptions.

When did the CMA essays stop?

May/June 2026 was the last window where English-exam candidates could still choose essays. From September/October 2026, case-based questions are the standard. The next window after that is January/February 2027, and it uses case-based questions.

Are case-based questions harder than essays?

IMA says the case-based questions test the same topics at the same difficulty level as the essays, and that only the format is different. They feel different because you select, drag and enter answers instead of writing paragraphs. IMA has not published pass-rate data for the new format, so any claim that they are easier or harder is an opinion.

How are CMA case-based questions scored?

The case-based section carries the same weight as the essay section, which is 25% of each part. IMA says it gives partial credit, as the essays did. The multiple-choice section is the other 75%, and you still need to score at least 50% on it to reach the case-based questions. IMA says there is no penalty for wrong answers.

How many case-based questions are on the CMA exam?

Two per part, so four across Part 1 and Part 2 together. They come after the 100 multiple-choice questions and make up 25% of each part’s score.

Is the CMA exam format different in the UAE and Saudi Arabia?

No. IMA lists the Middle East among the regions where case-based questions replaced essays on English exams, so candidates in the UAE and Saudi Arabia sit the same format as candidates in the Americas, Europe, Asia Pacific and Africa.

Can I still use my old essay-based CMA notes and classes?

Yes for the content, because the syllabus and topics did not change. But essay practice does not train drag-and-drop, select-from-a-list or numerical-entry questions, so add case-based practice. Ask your course how it covers them.

If I passed a part with essays, do I have to retake it?

No. A part you passed counts. IMA gives you a three-year window from entry to pass both parts, and the format change does not reset that. Check your own entry date in your IMA account.

Did the CMA exam fee or syllabus change with case-based questions?

No. IMA states that exam length, pricing and credential recognition remain unchanged, and the topics are the same. Only the second section of each part changed.
