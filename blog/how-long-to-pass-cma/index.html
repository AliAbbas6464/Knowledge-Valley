How Long Does It Take to Pass CMA USA? A Realistic Timeline for Working Professionals

How long does it take to pass CMA USA? Realistic timelines for working professionals: study hours per part, exam windows and total time to certification.

October 22, 2026 · 13 min read

Ask ten people how long does it take to pass CMA and you will get ten answers — six months, a year, two years, 'it depends.' In my research for this article, I went looking for the honest version of that answer: not the number coaching ads print in bold, and not the worst case either, but the timeline a working professional in Dubai, Riyadh or Doha can actually plan a life around. The short version is that the CMA duration question has a real answer, and it is mostly arithmetic. Two exam parts. A study-hours requirement the prep industry has measured for years. Three testing windows a year. One hard deadline from the IMA that nobody should ignore. Once you see those four numbers, the vague question turns into a calendar you can build.

THE SHORT ANSWER — Most working professionals pass both CMA parts in 9–15 months. Plan on roughly 150–200 study hours per part — 300–400 hours total — which at 8–10 hours a week means 4–5 months per part, including revision. The fastest honest route is about 6 months; the IMA's hard limit is 3 years.

Start With the Only Number That Matters: Study Hours

Calendars lie; hours don't. The reason candidates finish in wildly different times is that they study wildly different amounts per week — the underlying workload is far more consistent than the marketing suggests. Across the major prep providers — Gleim, Becker, Hock and others — the commonly cited planning figure is 150 to 200 hours of focused study per part. The IMA itself has long pointed candidates to at least 150 hours per part as a minimum, and providers who publish 'safe' targets (Gleim's worked estimates, for example) land around 170 hours for Part 1 once you include working through a full question bank and reviewing every wrong answer properly.

Treat 150 hours as the floor, not the target. That floor assumes a finance or accounting background, decent English, and study that is actually study — phone away, questions attempted, mistakes reviewed. If your degree is older, your job has kept you away from cost accounting for years, or you are studying in a second language at the end of a ten-hour workday, budget toward the 200-hour end. Two parts at 150–200 hours gives you the total CMA completion time in its truest form: 300–400 hours of your life. Everything else in this article is just deciding how to slice those hours across your weeks.

One more thing the hour-count explains: why Part 1 usually takes a little longer than Part 2. Part 1 (Financial Planning, Performance and Analytics) covers six sections, including the two heavyweights — Planning, Budgeting and Forecasting, and Performance Management — at 20% each. It is broad and calculation-dense. Part 2 (Strategic Financial Management) leans more on analysis and judgement, and candidates who work in finance often find their day job has pre-taught them part of it. Same exam length, slightly different mountain.

The Timeline Scenarios: 6, 12 or 18 Months?

Here is the arithmetic applied. Take the mid-range workload — say 175 hours per part, 350 in total — and divide by three realistic weekly paces for someone studying CMA while working full time. Then add what candidates always forget: booking lead time, the wait for results (IMA releases scores roughly six weeks after a testing window closes), and a revision buffer before each exam.

Read the table honestly against your own week. The aggressive column is real — people do finish CMA in six months — but it costs about 20 focused hours every week for half a year, which for a working finance professional means most evenings gone and weekends largely spoken for. The standard and steady columns are where the majority of working candidates in the Gulf actually land, and there is no shame in them: 12 months to a US professional credential, earned alongside a full-time job, is a strong outcome by any measure. What I would warn against is the fifth, unlisted scenario — starting at 20 hours a week, burning out by week six, and drifting to zero. That is how 12-month plans quietly become abandoned ones.

The 3-Year Rule: The IMA's Hard Deadline, Explained

Every timeline above sits inside one non-negotiable frame. From the date you pay the CMA program entrance fee and enter the program, the IMA gives you three years to pass both parts. Three years means six testing windows (the exams run in January–February, May–June and September–October each year). If the three years expire with only one part passed, that passed part expires too, and you re-enter the program — new entrance fee, new clock — and pass both parts again.

Three years sounds generous, and for most candidates it is. But notice what it does to the relaxed scenario: an 18-month plan that slips twice — one failed part, one window skipped for a work crisis — can find itself in year three with the clock suddenly loud. My practical rule from watching candidates plan: aim to be done in half the allowed time. Target 15–18 months maximum on paper, and the 3-year limit will never become a source of stress. Also remember the softer deadlines around it: your IMA membership must stay active, and the two years of qualifying work experience can be completed after passing — within seven years — so experience is almost never the thing that stretches a working professional's timeline. The exams are.

What Actually Stretches Timelines (It Is Rarely Intelligence)

When I look at why CMA journeys take two years instead of one, the same culprits appear every time — and raw ability is not one of them.

  • Retakes. With a global pass rate of roughly 45–50% per part, about half of all candidates sit at least one part twice. A retake cannot happen in the same window — you wait for the next one — so a single fail adds roughly four months once you count re-study and the results wait. This is the single biggest timeline variable, and it is why realistic plans build in one contingency window.
  • Work seasons — close and audit periods. Finance jobs run on a calendar that hates exams. Month-end close eats the first week of every month; quarter-ends and the annual audit consume far more. A candidate in external audit or group reporting who books an exam in their busiest season is not unlucky — they are mis-scheduled. Look at your company's calendar before you look at Prometric's.
  • Ramadan. During Ramadan, working hours shorten across the Gulf and energy patterns change completely — many professionals find late-evening study after taraweeh works beautifully, others find the month better spent on light revision than new material. Either way, if Ramadan falls inside your preparation window, plan for it deliberately instead of pretending it won't matter.
  • Life logistics. The Gulf runs on expatriate rhythms: annual leave home, family visits, school calendars, job changes between emirates or into Saudi. None of these are reasons to delay starting — but a plan that assumes 52 identical weeks will break. Build the breaks in.
  • The gap between parts. Starting Part 2 'when I feel like it' after passing Part 1 is where months evaporate. Momentum is a real asset: the study habit, the question technique, the discipline. Take two or three weeks off to celebrate, then start the next part while the machine is still warm.

A Worked Example: Sara's 11-Month Plan in Dubai

Let me make this concrete with a composite example drawn from the candidates I have researched — a senior accountant in Dubai, eight years into her career, working a standard week with a heavy first week of every month at close.

Sara enters the CMA program in early September and targets Part 1 in the January–February window. She studies 10 hours a week — an hour on three weekday evenings, a longer block on Saturday, a shorter one on Sunday — skipping study entirely during close week and compensating slightly in the weeks after. Part 1 takes her about 18 weeks and roughly 180 hours, including two full mock exams in the last fortnight. She sits in late January, gets her result in March, and takes exactly two weeks off.

Part 2 starts in mid-March at the same rhythm. Ramadan that year falls inside her preparation, so she front-loads the heavy Decision Analysis material (25% of Part 2) before it, and uses the Ramadan weeks for question practice only. She sits Part 2 in the September–October window — deliberately avoiding a May–June attempt that would have collided with her company's half-year reporting crunch. Total elapsed time: just under 11 months, start to second result, with one normal holiday and one very busy job held down throughout. Nothing about her pace was heroic. The plan worked because the exam dates were chosen around her work calendar, not against it.

The Fastest Realistic Path — and the 'CMA in 6 Months' Claim

Since the ads will find you anyway, let us deal with them directly. 'Pass CMA in 6 months!' is not a lie, exactly — it is a truth with the conditions removed. Six months requires roughly 20 study hours a week, sustained, plus first-attempt passes in both parts, plus windows that line up kindly. For a full-time student or someone between jobs, that is achievable. For a professional working 45–55 hours a week in a Gulf finance team, I have seen it attempted far more often than I have seen it finished — and the typical end state is a failed part, a dented confidence, and a longer total time than the steady plan would have taken.

The fastest realistic path for a working professional looks like this: 12–15 hours a week, Part 1 in the first available window after about three months of preparation, Part 2 started within a fortnight of the first exam, second sitting roughly four to five months later. That lands you at eight to ten months — genuinely fast, honestly earned, and compatible with keeping your job performance (and your manager's goodwill) intact. Optimize for passing first time, not for the headline. A first-attempt pass at month ten beats a failed sprint at month six and a pass at month fourteen, every single time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to pass CMA for a working professional?

Most working professionals take 9–15 months to pass both parts. At 8–10 study hours a week, each part needs roughly 4–5 months including revision; at 12 hours a week, candidates often finish both parts within a year.

How many study hours does each CMA part need?

Prep providers commonly cite 150–200 hours per part — the IMA points to at least 150 — so plan on 300–400 hours total. Candidates with recent, relevant finance experience may land near the bottom of the range; career changers should budget near the top.

What is the maximum time allowed to complete CMA?

Three years from the date you enter the CMA program (pay the entrance fee). If you have not passed both parts by then, any passed part expires and you must re-enter the program and pass both parts again.

Can I really finish CMA in 6 months?

Yes, but only at around 20 focused hours a week with first-attempt passes — realistic for full-time students or candidates on a career break. For someone working full time, 8–12 months is the fast-but-honest range.

Which part should I take first, and does it affect the timeline?

Most candidates take Part 1 first; it is broader and slightly longer to prepare. You may take the parts in either order, and taking the part closest to your current job first can shorten preparation because your daily work reinforces the material.

If I fail a part, how much time does a retake add?

You cannot retake a part in the same testing window, so a fail typically adds about four months: wait for the next window, top up your preparation (usually 40–80 focused hours on your weak sections), and sit again. Roughly half of candidates need at least one retake, so a good plan holds a contingency window.

Does the work-experience requirement delay my certification?

It rarely delays working professionals. You need two years of qualifying experience, but you can complete it after passing the exams — within seven years of passing — so for most people already working in finance, the requirement is satisfied before or soon after the exams.

Final Verdict

If this article has one sentence worth keeping, it is this: CMA takes about a year of a working professional's life when it is planned in hours and protected from the predictable collisions — close weeks, audit season, Ramadan, burnout — and it takes two years or forever when it is left to good intentions. The candidates who finish are not the ones who studied the most hours in a single week. They are the ones who picked a pace their real life could sustain, booked exam windows around their work calendar, and treated the IMA's 3-year deadline as a backstop rather than a plan.
