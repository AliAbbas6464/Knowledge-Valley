CMA Salary in Saudi Arabia in 2026: What CMAs Earn in Riyadh and Jeddah

CMA salary in Saudi Arabia in 2026: monthly pay in Riyadh and Jeddah by experience level, allowances on top, Saudization reality and Vision 2030 demand.

October 22, 2026 · 15 min read

Of all the questions I research for CMA students, 'what will I actually earn?' is the one people ask quietly and care about most. Ask it about Saudi Arabia and you will get ten different answers, most of them copied from each other. So let me do this properly. The CMA salary in Saudi Arabia is, by Gulf standards, one of the strongest stories in finance right now — because the Kingdom is in the middle of the largest building and diversification programme on the planet, and every giga-project, hospital group, retail chain and factory rising under Vision 2030 needs people who can budget, cost, forecast and control. That is precisely the CMA skill set. For this article I went through the current recruiter salary guides and live Riyadh and Jeddah listings and built the picture below: what CMAs earn at each level, how the city changes the number, what the allowances stacked on top are really worth, and what Saudization means if you are an expat reading this. All figures are monthly, in Saudi riyals, and — as everything in Saudi — income-tax free.

THE SHORT ANSWER — In 2026, a CMA in Saudi Arabia typically earns SAR 8,000–14,000 a month early in their career, SAR 15,000–25,000 as a senior or management accountant, SAR 25,000–40,000 as a finance manager, and SAR 38,000–55,000 or more as a financial controller — tax-free, with housing and transport allowances usually built into the package.

First, a Word on Method: Indicative Ranges, Not Promises

Salary articles fail readers in two ways: they invent precise numbers, or they copy one guide without asking who the guide was describing. I have tried to avoid both. The ranges in this article are synthesised mainly from the Hays Saudi Arabia Salary Guide 2025 (Accountancy & Finance), cross-checked against other recruiter market data and live job listings in Riyadh and Jeddah in October 2026. Where guides disagree — and they do — I have used the overlap, not the most exciting number.

One Saudi-specific point matters before any table: when a Saudi employer quotes a 'salary', they usually mean the total fixed monthly package. Your basic salary is often only 60–70% of that figure, with housing and transport allowances making up the rest. Every figure below is a total monthly package unless I say otherwise. Treat the ranges as a map for negotiation and career planning — your actual offer will move with company size, sector, and how scarce your exact experience is that quarter.

A few honest observations on that table. First, it lines up well with the hard guide data: Hays puts the general-market Finance Manager band at SAR 25,000–45,000 and Financial Controller at SAR 40,000–60,000, with CFOs at large employers running from SAR 90,000 upward — the top rows above are for the much larger population of mid-size companies, not the PIF-ecosystem giants. Second, notice where the CMA premium does its work. A generic 'accountant' in the Saudi market spans roughly SAR 10,000–20,000 in the Hays data, and plenty of junior accounting roles advertise below that. The CMA does not magically lift a junior into the manager band — but it consistently places you at the top of your band, and it is the key that opens the next one. Third, the jump that matters most is the one from senior accountant to finance manager: that is roughly a SAR 10,000-a-month step, and in listing after listing it is gated on exactly what CMA certifies — budgeting ownership, cost control, and reporting to management rather than only to auditors.

Riyadh vs Jeddah: Same Job, Different Number

Saudi Arabia is not one salary market; it is at least three. Riyadh is the capital, home to government, the Public Investment Fund ecosystem, regional headquarters (which Saudi rules now require for firms wanting government contracts), and the management teams of the giga-projects. It pays the top of every band in the table above, and finance manager roles in Riyadh's construction and real-estate groups regularly advertise at SAR 25,000 and above. Jeddah is the Kingdom's historic commercial port — trading houses, retail groups, logistics, food, and Red Sea tourism — and it typically pays around 10% below Riyadh for the same title, occasionally more at senior levels. The third market, the Eastern Province (Khobar, Dammam, Jubail), is energy and industry: fewer roles, but petrochemical and industrial employers there pay Riyadh-level packages for experienced plant and project finance people.

Before Riyadh's premium seduces you, run the living-cost side too. Jeddah rents — especially for family housing — have historically run below Riyadh's, and the lifestyle is more relaxed. A SAR 3,000 monthly pay gap can disappear entirely into a Riyadh rent cheque. Negotiate on the total package and the housing component, city by city, rather than chasing the biggest headline number.

The CMA Premium: What the Letters Actually Add

Does the certificate itself raise pay, or does it just follow people who were going to earn more anyway? IMA's own global salary research has consistently found that CMAs earn a substantial premium over non-certified peers, and the Saudi listing market shows you the mechanism. Scan Riyadh finance-manager adverts and a pattern repeats: the roles at the top of the band name a professional certification — 'SOCPA, CMA, CPA or ACCA preferred' is a phrase you will meet again and again — and they pair that requirement with budgeting, forecasting and ERP responsibilities. The uncertified equivalent role, same title, same city, is usually a transaction-processing job in disguise, and it is priced like one.

My reading of the market: at junior level the CMA premium is modest — the difference between the middle and the top of the band. From senior accountant upward it becomes structural. The roles paying SAR 25,000 a month and beyond are management-accounting roles — planning, costing, performance analysis — and employers use CMA (alongside CPA and ACCA) as the screening shortcut for them. When job-hunting, search by title family, not by the certificate alone: management accountant, FP&A analyst, cost controller, finance manager. That is where your premium is hiding.

Why Demand Keeps Rising: Vision 2030 Is a Finance Hiring Programme

It is hard to overstate how much hiring the Vision 2030 programme generates in finance. NEOM, the Red Sea and Amaala tourism projects, Qiddiya, Diriyah Gate, ROSHN housing — each giga-project is a small economy with contractors, subcontractors, operators and suppliers, and every one of them needs project costing, budget control and management reporting at a scale Saudi Arabia has never had to staff before. CMA demand in Saudi Vision 2030 sectors is not a slogan; it is a staffing table.

The sectors I see hiring CMAs hardest, in rough order:

  • Construction & giga-projects: the giga-projects and the contractors building them need cost controllers and project accountants who can run earned-value and budget-variance discipline on multi-billion-riyal programmes.
  • Healthcare: private hospital groups expanding under the health-transformation programme need costing per procedure, per department, per insurer — classic management accounting.
  • Retail & consumer: national retail and F&B chains scaling across the Kingdom live and die on margin per store; their finance teams are built around management accountants.
  • Manufacturing: industrial localisation is pushing factories to cost products properly for the first time — standard costing, variance analysis, pricing support.
  • Energy & petrochemicals: the traditional heavyweight; fewer openings, but the highest packages for experienced finance staff, especially in the Eastern Province.

Layered on top is a compliance tailwind: ZATCA's e-invoicing rollout (Fatoora) and maturing VAT and zakat administration mean even mid-size Saudi companies now need finance staff who can run controlled, system-based accounting. That, too, is CMA territory — Part 1 of the syllabus covers internal controls and technology for a reason.

Saudization and Nitaqat: The Honest Picture for Expat CMAs

If you are an expat, you will have heard that Saudization is 'closing' Saudi finance jobs. The reality is more specific, and worth understanding precisely. Under the Nitaqat programme, companies are banded by the share of Saudi nationals they employ, and their band controls their ability to issue new work visas. On top of the general bands, the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development has added profession-level localisation: for accounting roles, establishments with five or more workers in designated accounting titles must meet a Saudization target that began at 40% in October 2025 and rises in annual steps (50% from October 2026). Always check the current requirement on the Qiwa platform — the rules are updated in phases.

What does that mean in practice? Three things. First, it is a quota on job titles inside larger accounting teams, not a ban on expatriates — the same companies still hire experienced foreign finance staff, and they still sponsor iqamas for them. Second, the pressure lands hardest on junior, generic accounting roles, which are increasingly filled by Saudi graduates; expat demand concentrates where experience is genuinely scarce — senior management accounting, FP&A, project finance, ERP implementation, and team leadership. Third, expats who mentor and build Saudi teams have become more valuable, not less: a CMA who can run the function and train the next Saudi finance manager is exactly who a compliance-conscious employer wants. Practical advice: before accepting an offer, it is reasonable to ask about the company's Nitaqat band, and Arabic — even conversational — is a genuine differentiator at interview.

Count the Whole Package: Allowances, Flights and End-of-Service

Newcomers to Saudi chronically undervalue offers because they compare a basic salary against a Dubai total package. Do the full arithmetic. A typical Saudi package for a finance professional is built like this: a basic salary (often 60–70% of the total), a housing allowance commonly around 25% of basic (or company-provided accommodation, still standard with larger employers), and a transport allowance around 10% of basic or a company car. Medical insurance for you and your dependants is mandatory for the employer to provide, and annual air tickets home for the family are a standard line in professional packages. Performance bonuses exist in larger companies and giga-project employers, though they are less formulaic than in banking.

Then there is the quiet wealth-builder: end-of-service benefits. Under Saudi labour law, your award accrues with every year of service — roughly half a month's wage per year for the first five years and a month per year after that, on resignation terms that improve with tenure. Stay eight or ten years with one employer and you leave with a lump sum many expats describe as their real savings plan, on top of a tax-free salary. When you compare a Riyadh offer with one from anywhere else, compare decade outcomes, not monthly ones.

What Actually Moves You Up the Bands

Across the guides and listings, the same levers appear:

  • ERP depth: SAP S/4HANA and Oracle dominate Saudi large-employer finance; hands-on ERP experience — not 'exposure' — is priced into offers.
  • FP&A ownership: budgeting, forecasting and variance commentary for management. This is the core CMA skill and the core of the finance-manager pay band.
  • Sector costing: project costing experience is scarce and giga-project employers pay for it; healthcare and retail costing are close behind.
  • CMA plus experience, in that order: employers repeatedly pair the certification with 5+ years of relevant experience; the letters open the door, the track record sets the number.
  • Arabic: not a formal requirement in most private-sector finance roles, but it widens your shortlist chances, especially in Saudi-owned groups.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average CMA salary in Saudi Arabia?

There is no single official average, but across the 2025–26 recruiter guides and live listings, a mid-career CMA in Saudi Arabia (senior/management accountant level) typically lands between SAR 15,000 and SAR 25,000 a month as a total package. Finance managers cluster around SAR 25,000–40,000 and financial controllers around SAR 38,000–55,000, with Riyadh at the top of each range.

Is CMA in demand in Saudi Arabia?

Yes — and the demand is structural, not cyclical. Vision 2030 giga-projects, healthcare expansion, retail growth and industrial localisation all need budgeting, costing and performance-management skills, which is exactly what CMA certifies. Finance-manager and cost-control listings in Riyadh regularly name CMA among the preferred qualifications.

Does Saudization mean expats can no longer get accounting jobs in Saudi Arabia?

No. Saudization sets quotas — for example, larger accounting teams must meet rising Saudi-national percentages under the profession-level rules phased in from October 2025 — but it is not a ban. Expat hiring continues, concentrated in senior, specialist and leadership roles where experienced candidates are scarce. Always verify the current rules on the official Qiwa platform, as targets rise in phases.

Which city pays CMAs more — Riyadh or Jeddah?

Riyadh, in almost every comparison: it hosts government, the PIF ecosystem, regional headquarters and giga-project management, and typically pays around 10% more than Jeddah for the same role. Jeddah partly compensates with lower living costs, especially rent. The Eastern Province matches Riyadh pay in energy and industry roles.

Is CMA salary in Saudi Arabia tax-free?

Yes. Saudi Arabia levies no personal income tax on employment income, so the monthly package figures in this article are what lands in your account (before your own spending, and subject to social-insurance rules that differ for Saudi and expatriate employees).

What allowances come on top of salary in Saudi Arabia?

Strictly, the allowances are usually inside the quoted total package: housing (commonly ~25% of basic, or company accommodation), transport (~10% of basic or a car), mandatory employer-provided medical insurance, and annual air tickets at most professional employers. End-of-service benefits accrue separately under labour law and are paid when you leave.

CMA or SOCPA — which is better for working in Saudi Arabia?

They do different jobs. SOCPA is the Saudi professional body and its fellowship matters most for audit and statutory roles in the Kingdom. CMA is the corporate route — management accounting, FP&A and finance leadership inside companies — and is the more portable credential if you later move elsewhere in the Gulf. Many senior finance professionals in Saudi hold an international certification and build SOCPA-recognised local knowledge alongside it.

My Verdict

If I had to compress this research into one quotable line, it would be this: Saudi Arabia in 2026 rewards the CMA twice — once in the salary band itself, which now reaches SAR 40,000 a month at finance-manager level in Riyadh, and again in the package stacked around it: housing, transport, flights and an end-of-service award, all on a tax-free income. No other Gulf market is adding finance jobs at this pace, and the roles being added — project costing, FP&A, management reporting — read like the CMA syllabus table of contents.

The catch is the same one I raise in every salary article: the band belongs to the role, not the certificate. A CMA who can run a budget cycle, speak the language of variance and value, and operate an ERP like SAP will negotiate at the top of these ranges; a certificate without that evidence negotiates in the middle.
